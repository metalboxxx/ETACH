================================================================================
ENGLISH GRAMMAR SKILL TAXONOMY — CEFR A1 TO B2
For use by AI agents: skill identification, lesson building, and student evaluation
================================================================================

SOURCES:
- Cambridge YLE Handbook 2024
- English Profile Grammar Syllabus compiled from EU official data (A1–B2)
- ExamEnglish CEFR grammar level reference
- Test-English grammar table of contents

CEFR LEVEL SCALE:
- Pre-A1 = Beginner (below A1)
- A1     = Elementary
- A2     = Pre-Intermediate
- B1     = Intermediate
- B2     = Upper-Intermediate

SKILL ID FORMAT: GR-[CONCEPT_CODE]-[LEVEL]
- CONCEPT_CODE is a 3-letter abbreviation of the grammar concept.
- LEVEL is the CEFR level at which this version of the skill is introduced.
- Same concept code across different levels = same concept, expanded scope.
- Skills are CUMULATIVE: a B1 student is expected to have mastered all A1 and A2 skills.

MASTERY LEVELS (applies to every skill ID):
- Mastery 1: Can apply the formula mechanically with simple, familiar vocabulary.
- Mastery 2: Can apply the grammar correctly in real communicative context with appropriate vocabulary and sentence complexity for the CEFR level. This is the PASS threshold.
- Mastery 3: Can apply the grammar correctly in tricky or misleading situations designed to confuse (e.g. near-identical forms, common error traps, mixed tenses).

A student must reach Mastery Level 2 on a skill to be considered as having "passed" that skill.

================================================================================
SECTION 1: PRESENT SIMPLE (PSV)
================================================================================

GR-PSV-A1 | Present Simple — Affirmative Forms | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Affirmative statements only: "I eat", "She eats", "They play".
- Third-person singular -s ending (eats, reads, goes).
- Basic adverbs of frequency in mid position: always, usually, sometimes, never.
- "To be" as a linking verb in present simple: "He is happy", "They are friends".
NOTE: Negative and question forms are NOT yet expected at this level.
Example sentences:
  "Nick is happy."
  "She always eats lunch at school."
  "My cat sleeps a lot."

GR-PSV-A2 | Present Simple — Negatives, Questions and Full System | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Negative forms: don't / doesn't + base verb. "I don't like eggs." "She doesn't play tennis."
- Question forms: Do you...? Does she...? "Does he walk to school?"
- Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, she doesn't.
- Indirect/embedded questions: "I want to know what time the shop opens."
- Third-person question forms fully mastered.
- Mental process verbs (know, think, believe, want) in present simple.
- Present simple in conditional if-clauses (see GR-CND-A2).
NOTE: This is the completion of the present simple system.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: PRESENT CONTINUOUS (PCV)
================================================================================

GR-PCV-A1 | Present Continuous — Affirmative Forms | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Affirmative form only: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing. "The cat is sleeping." "What are you doing?"
- Actions happening right now.
- Basic -ing spelling rules (running, sitting, playing).
NOTE: Negative and question forms introduced at A2. Future reference NOT expected.
Example: "What are you doing? The cat's sleeping."

GR-PCV-A2 | Present Continuous — Negatives, Questions and Future Use | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Negative forms: am/is/are + not + verb-ing. "She isn't watching TV."
- Question forms: Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing? "Are they coming?"
- Short answers: Yes, she is. / No, they aren't.
- Present continuous for FUTURE ARRANGEMENTS: "I'm meeting my friend tomorrow."
- Temporary situations (contrast with present simple habits).
NOTE: The shift to future meaning is a key expansion at A2.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: TO BE (TBE)
================================================================================

GR-TBE-A1 | To Be — Full Present Paradigm | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Full affirmative paradigm: I am, you are, he/she/it is, we/they are.
- Contractions: I'm, you're, he's, she's, it's, we're, they're.
- Negatives: I'm not, she isn't, they aren't.
- Yes/No questions: "Is that your sister?" + short answers: "Yes, it is." / "No, it isn't."
- Wh- questions with "to be": "Who is that man?" "Where is Alex?"
Example: "Nick is happy." "Is that your sister? Yes, it is."

GR-TBE-A2 | To Be — Past Forms | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Was / Were in affirmative, negative, and question forms.
- "Was he at school yesterday?" "They weren't happy."
- Short answers: "Yes, she was." / "No, they weren't."
NOTE: Listed separately because past "to be" unlocks past simple and past continuous.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: PAST SIMPLE (PST)
================================================================================

GR-PST-A1 | Past Simple — Affirmative Forms | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Affirmative only with the most common irregular verbs: went, had, said, got, saw, ate, came, made, took, gave, wore, bought, brought, thought.
- Regular verbs in affirmative: walked, played, watched, cooked.
- Time markers: yesterday, last week, last Monday, on Saturday.
Example: "We went to the park yesterday." "Her father cooked lunch on Friday."
NOTE: Negative and question forms are NOT yet required at A1.

GR-PST-A2 | Past Simple — Negatives, Questions and Extended Irregulars | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Negative forms: didn't + base verb. "We didn't see the pirate."
- Question forms: Did + subject + base verb? "Did you go to the cinema?"
- Short answers: "Yes, I did." / "No, she didn't."
- Extended set of irregular verbs (beyond A1 core set).
- Wh- questions in past: "Where did you go?" "What did she say?"
Example: "Did you go to the cinema? Yes, I did. We didn't see the pirate."

================================================================================
SECTION 5: PAST CONTINUOUS (PCT)
================================================================================

GR-PCT-A2 | Past Continuous | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Form: was/were + verb-ing. Affirmative, negative, question.
- Ongoing background action interrupted by a past simple event: "I was cooking when he arrived."
- Setting the scene in a story: "It was raining. The children were playing inside."
- Question form: "What were you doing at 5pm?"
NOTE: Full narrative contrast with past simple is further developed at B1(see GR-NAR-B1).

================================================================================
SECTION 6: PRESENT PERFECT (PPF)
================================================================================

GR-PPF-A2 | Present Perfect — Experience and Recent Events | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Form: have/has + past participle.
- Basic uses: recent events ("She has just left."), and experience ("I have seen that film.").
- Key adverbs: just, already, yet (basic), ever, never.
- Negative: "He hasn't finished." Question: "Have you ever been to London?"
NOTE: Contrast with past simple (since / for) is NOT yet required. That distinction is B1.

GR-PPF-B1 | Present Perfect — Contrast with Past Simple and Continuous Form | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Contrast of present perfect vs. past simple: "I have lived here for ten years." vs. "I lived there in 2010."
- Time expressions: since (a point in time), for (a duration).
- Present perfect continuous: have/has + been + verb-ing. "She has been waiting for an hour."
- Use for actions that started in the past and continue now.

================================================================================
SECTION 7: PAST PERFECT (PPT)
================================================================================

GR-PPT-B1 | Past Perfect Simple | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Form: had + past participle.
- Sequence of two past events: "She had already left when I arrived."
- In reported speech backshift: "He said he had seen it."
- With by the time: "By the time we arrived, the film had started."

GR-PPT-B2 | Past Perfect Continuous | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Form: had been + verb-ing.
- Duration of an action before another past event: "She had been working for three hours before she took a break."
- Explaining the cause of a past state: "He was tired because he had been running."

================================================================================
SECTION 8: FUTURE FORMS (FUT)
================================================================================

GR-FUT-A1 | Future — Will and Going To (Basic Introduction) | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Will / shall: simple affirmative statements. "I will help you." "It will be fun."
- Going to: plans and intentions. "I'm going to eat pizza tonight."
- Both introduced at surface level — no contrast between them required at A1.
Note: "going to" for plans and intentions, "will"/"shall" for future.

GR-FUT-A2 | Future — Will vs Going To Contrast | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Will for spontaneous decisions and offers: "I'll help you carry that."
- Going to for prior plans and predictions based on evidence: "Look at those clouds — it's going to rain."
- Present continuous for confirmed future arrangements: "I'm meeting her at 5pm." (see also GR-PCV-A2)
- Will negative: "She won't come." Will questions: "Will you be there?"

GR-FUT-B1 | Future — Continuous Form and Evidence-Based Predictions | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Future continuous: will be + verb-ing. "I'll be waiting at the gate." "This time tomorrow, she'll be flying."
- Will and going to for predictions, both with and without evidence.
- "Shall" for suggestions and offers in questions: "Shall we go?"

GR-FUT-B2 | Future — Perfect Forms and Advanced Expressions | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Future perfect: will have + past participle. "By Friday, I will have finished the report."
- Future perfect continuous: will have been + verb-ing. "By next year, she'll have been teaching for a decade."
- Other future expressions: be about to, be due to, be on the verge of. "The train is about to leave."

================================================================================
SECTION 9: CONDITIONALS (CND)
================================================================================

GR-CND-A2 | Conditionals — Zero and First (Real Conditions) | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Zero conditional: If + present simple, present simple. General truths. "If you heat water to 100°C, it boils."
- First conditional: If + present simple, will + base verb. Real/likely future. "If it rains, I'll stay home."
- "Unless" as an alternative to "if not": "Unless you hurry, you'll miss the bus."
Note: zero and first conditional confirmed.

GR-CND-B1 | Conditionals — Second and Third (Imagined and Past Regret) | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Second conditional: If + past simple, would + base verb. Imagined/unlikely situations. "If I had money, I would travel more."
- "If I were you, I would..." (fixed advisory form).
- Third conditional: If + past perfect, would have + past participle. Past regret/impossibility. "If I had studied harder, I would have passed."
- "Could" and "might" as alternatives to "would" in the result clause.

GR-CND-B2 | Conditionals — Mixed Forms and Wish Structures | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Mixed conditionals: If + past perfect, would + base verb (past cause, present result). "If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now."
- Wish / If only for regret: "I wish I had more time." (present wish) "I wish I had studied harder." (past regret)
- Wish + would for expressing irritation or desire for change: "I wish he would stop talking."

================================================================================
SECTION 10: MODAL VERBS (MOD)
================================================================================

GR-MOD-A1 | Modals — Can and Could (Ability, Permission, Polite Requests) | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Can for ability: "The baby can wave." "I can swim."
- Can't for lack of ability: "Penguins can't fly."
- Can for requests/permission: "Can I have some birthday cake?"
- Could for past ability: "When I was young, I could run very fast."
- Could for polite requests: "Could you help me, please?"
- Imperative forms as instructions: "Eat your lunch!" "Don't run!"

GR-MOD-A2 | Modals — Obligation, Advice, Permission and Surface Possibility | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Must / mustn't: strong obligation and prohibition. "He must do his homework." "You mustn't give the rabbit cheese."
- Have to / don't have to / had to: obligation and absence of obligation. "I've got to go." "He had to draw a whale."
- Should / shouldn't: advice. "You should see a doctor." "You shouldn't eat so much sugar."
- May for permission (formal): "May I open the window?" = polite request.
- Shall for offers and suggestions: "Shall I help you?" "Shall we go?"
- Would for preferences and polite requests: "I'd like a coffee." "Would you like to come?"
- Might for surface/recognition-level possibility: "It might rain." "She might come to the party."
  Students at A2 only need to recognise that might = something could possibly happen.
  No distinction from "may" required. No past forms. No deduction use.
NOTE: Full modal possibility/probability distinction (may vs. might) and deduction use (must/can't) are B1 requirements — see GR-MOD-B1.
Examples: "He must do his homework." "You mustn't give the rabbit cheese." "I've got to go." "He had to draw a whale."
Note: "might" used to show something will or will not happen.

GR-MOD-B1 | Modals — Possibility, Deduction and Past Modal Forms | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- May vs. might: may (stronger possibility), might (weaker/more uncertain possibility). "She may be at home." "He might know the answer."
- Must for logical deduction (certainty): "He must be tired — he worked all day."
- Can't for logical impossibility: "That can't be right."
- Should have / might have / could have: past modal speculation and criticism.
  "You should have told me." "She might have missed the train." "He could have called."
- Will probably: "She'll probably be late."
- Modals + continuous: "She must be sleeping."

GR-MOD-B2 | Modals — Past Deduction, Speculation and Habitual Past | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Can't have + past participle: past logical impossibility. "He can't have done it — he was with me."
- Must have + past participle: past logical certainty. "She must have left already."
- Might have / could have: past possibility. "They might have taken a wrong turn."
- Needn't have + past participle: something done unnecessarily. "You needn't have cooked so much."
- Would for past habits and routines (different from "used to"): "Every summer, we would go to the coast."

================================================================================
SECTION 11: PASSIVE VOICE (PAS)
================================================================================

GR-PAS-B1 | Passive Voice — Present and Past Simple Forms | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Present simple passive: am/is/are + past participle. "English is spoken in many countries." "The letter is written."
- Past simple passive: was/were + past participle. "The book was published in 1990."
- By-agent (optional): "The cake was made by my mother."
- Question forms: "Is it sold here?" "Was the window broken?"
- Negative: "The report wasn't finished."

GR-PAS-B2 | Passive Voice — Complex Tenses and Reporting Structures | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Passive across other tenses: present continuous passive ("It is being repaired."), present perfect passive ("It has been done."), future passive ("It will be delivered tomorrow.").
- Passive of reporting verbs: "It is said that..." / "He is believed to be..." / "Scientists are thought to have discovered..."
- Get-passive (informal): "She got fired." "He got hurt."
- Passive with two objects: "She was given an award." / "The prize was awarded to her."

================================================================================
SECTION 12: REPORTED SPEECH (RPT)
================================================================================

GR-RPT-B1 | Reported Speech — Statements with Tense Backshift | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Reporting statements with tense backshift: "She said she was tired." "He told me he had finished."
- Basic reporting verbs: said, told.
- Pronoun and time reference shifts: "now" → "then", "today" → "that day", "I" → "he/she".
- Reporting questions (indirect): "She asked me where I lived." "He wanted to know if I was ready."
NOTE: A2 introduces "say" and "tell" as vocabulary, but backshifted reported speech structure is B1.

GR-RPT-B2 | Reported Speech — Commands, Questions and Extended Reporting Verbs | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Reported commands and requests: "She told me to sit down." "He asked me not to be late."
- Reporting verbs with various patterns: suggested (+ -ing), refused (+ to-inf), admitted (+ -ing), warned (+ not to), denied (+ -ing), advised (+ to-inf), promised (+ to-inf), offered (+ to-inf).
- "She suggested going to the cinema." "He admitted taking the money."

================================================================================
SECTION 13: RELATIVE CLAUSES (REL)
================================================================================

GR-REL-A2 | Relative Clauses — Defining Clauses (who, which, that, where) | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Defining relative clauses with who (people), which (things), that (people or things), where (places).
- "The man who lives next door is a teacher."
- "This is the book that I told you about."
- "That's the shop where I bought my shoes."
- No commas. The clause is essential to identify the noun.
NOTE: Confirmed in English Profile A2 grammar as "defining relative clause, subject, with 'who' and 'which'".
Example: "Taji is the boy who sits next to Zac."

GR-REL-B1 | Relative Clauses — Non-Defining Clauses | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Non-defining relative clauses (with commas): "My brother, who lives in Paris, is an architect."
- The clause adds extra, non-essential information.
- Who, which, where — the pronoun cannot be omitted.
- "The Eiffel Tower, which is in Paris, attracts millions of visitors."

GR-REL-B2 | Relative Clauses — Whose, Whom and Reduced Forms | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Whose: "The author whose book I read is coming to the school."
- Whom (formal): "The man whom I met was very kind."
- Reduced relative clauses (participle phrases replacing relative clause): "The woman sitting by the window is my aunt." (= who is sitting)
- Relative clauses with prepositions: "The person to whom I spoke was very helpful."

================================================================================
SECTION 14: ARTICLES (ART)
================================================================================

GR-ART-A1 | Articles — Basic Use of A, An, The and Zero Article | A1
Introduced at: Pre-A1
What is tested:
- a/an with singular countable nouns: "It's a banana." "This is an apple."
- the for specific reference: "Put the hat on the boy's head."
- No article (zero article) with proper nouns: names, countries.
- a/an to describe jobs: "She's a doctor."
- a/an vs. some (basic): "I want some milk."

GR-ART-A2 | Articles — Extended Use with Superlatives, Unique Reference and General Meaning | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- The with superlatives: "She is the best student."
- The with unique reference: "the sun", "the moon", "the internet".
- Zero article for general/uncountable meaning: "I like music." "Water is important." (vs. "I like the music in this film.")
- A/an with adjective + noun: "It was a beautiful day."
- Complex noun phrases: determiner + uncountable noun, possessive + noun.

================================================================================
SECTION 15: NOUNS AND PRONOUNS (NPN)
================================================================================

GR-NPN-A1 | Nouns and Pronouns — Core Set (Personal, Possessive, Demonstrative) | A1
Introduced at: Pre-A1
What is tested:
- Singular and plural nouns including irregular forms: mice, children, people, teeth, feet.
- Countable and uncountable nouns (basic): water, rice, milk.
- Personal pronouns (subject): I, you, he, she, it, we, they.
- Personal pronouns (object): me, you, him, her, it, us, them.
- Possessive adjectives: my, your, his, her, its, our, their.
- Possessive pronouns: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.
- Possessive 's: "Mike's car", "the dog's name".
- Demonstrative pronouns: this, that, these, those.
- "One" as a substitute pronoun: "I'd like one." "Which is the red one?"
Note: "Pronouns including demonstrative, personal, and possessive interrogative pronouns and 'one'."

GR-NPN-A2 | Nouns and Pronouns — Indefinite, Reflexive and Impersonal Forms | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Indefinite pronouns: something, anything, nothing, someone, anyone, no one, everybody, everything.
- "There is something on the table." "Did you see anyone?"
- Impersonal "you": "How do you spell that?" "You should always check your work."
- "It" as empty/preparatory subject: "It's raining." "It was a good idea to come."
- Reflexive pronouns: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves.
NOTE: English Profile places reflexive pronouns at A2 with "same subject and object" use.

GR-NPN-B1 | Nouns and Pronouns — Question Tags and Substitution with So | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Question tags (full range): "It's cold, isn't it?" "You don't know, do you?" "She can swim, can't she?" "They've finished, haven't they?"
- Generic/impersonal "one" (formal): "One should always be polite."
- Substitution with "so": "I think so." "I hope so." / "So do I." "Neither do I."

SECTION 16: QUANTIFIERS (QNT)
================================================================================

GR-QNT-A1 | Quantifiers — Basic (Some, Any, How Much, How Many) | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- How much / how many (with uncountable and countable nouns).
- A lot of (basic use).
- Some / any: "I want some milk." "Have you got any biscuits?"
- Very + adjective: "It's very cold."
- A/an, a few, a little (basic introduction).

GR-QNT-A2 | Quantifiers — Much, Many, Too Much, Enough and Most | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Much / many / a lot of: with countable and uncountable nouns in positive, negative, and question forms.
- A little / a few / little / few (with meaning distinction).
- Too much / too many: "There is too much sugar." "There are too many people."
- Enough: "Is there enough food?" "He isn't old enough."
- Most / most of: "Most children like playing." "Most of my friends live nearby."

GR-QNT-B1 | Quantifiers — All, Both, Either, Neither, None and Other | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- All / both / either / neither / none (with correct agreement and use).
  "All students must attend." "Both answers are correct." "Neither option is good."
- All of / both of / none of + pronoun: "All of them left." "Neither of us knew."
- Any / no / none in complex sentences: "There was no food left." "None of them came."
- Another / other / others / the other / the others: "Give me another chance." "The others stayed."

SECTION 17: ADJECTIVES AND ADVERBS (ADJ)
================================================================================

GR-ADJ-A1 | Adjectives and Adverbs — Basic Comparison and Adverbs of Manner | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Adjective position: attributive ("a big dog") and predicative ("The dog is big").
- Basic comparative: -er than / more ... than. "Your house is bigger than mine."
- Basic superlative: the -est / the most. "Anna is my best friend."
- Adverbs of manner (basic): slowly, loudly, quickly. "He sang loudly."
- Adverbs of frequency: always, usually, sometimes, never — in mid position.
Examples: "Your house is bigger than mine." "My brother reads more quickly than my sister." "He sang loudly."

GR-ADJ-A2 | Adjectives and Adverbs — Irregular Comparisons and Degree Adverbs | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Irregular comparatives and superlatives: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst; far → further → the furthest.
- Comparative structures: as ... as ("She is as tall as her brother."), not as ... as.
- Adverbial phrases of time, place, and frequency with correct word order.
- Degree adverbs with adjectives: quite, fairly, very, really, extremely.
- Adverbs of stance and certainty: probably, definitely, certainly.
- "No longer", "any longer", "anymore": "She doesn't live here anymore."

GR-ADJ-B1 | Adjectives and Adverbs — Intensifiers, Compound Adjectives and -ed/-ing Forms | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Intensifiers: too + adjective ("It's too hot."), enough + noun ("There isn't enough time."), so + adjective + that ("It was so cold that we stayed inside.").
- Compound adjectives with numbers: "a two-hour trip", "a ten-year-old boy".
- -ed / -ing adjectives: "I'm bored." "It was a boring lesson." (adjectives from verbs: excited/exciting, interested/interesting, surprised/surprising).
- "Such a / such": "It was such a good film." "She has such patience."

GR-ADJ-B2 | Adjectives and Adverbs — Subtle Distinctions and Participle Modifiers | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Subtle adjective vs. adverb distinctions: "She spoke quietly" vs. "She is quiet." Hard/hardly, late/lately, high/highly, near/nearly.
- Participle clauses used as adjective modifiers: "The man standing by the door is my uncle." (present participle) / "The letter written in 1945 was found." (past participle).
- Position of multiple adjectives (opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material): "a beautiful small antique round green French silver box."

================================================================================
SECTION 18: VERB PATTERNS — GERUNDS AND INFINITIVES (VPT)
================================================================================

GR-VPT-A1 | Verb Patterns — Like/Love/Hate + -ing and Want/Would Like + Infinitive | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- Verb + -ing with: like, love, hate, enjoy. "I like swimming." "She loves reading."
- Gerund as subject (basic): "Swimming is good for you."
- Would like + to-infinitive: "I'd like to go home."
- Want + to-infinitive: "I want to go home."
Example: "I want to go home." "He started to laugh." (verb + infinitive)
Note: "ing forms as nouns: Swimming is good."

GR-VPT-A2 | Verb Patterns — Expanded Verb + To-Infinitive and Gerund Patterns | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Verb + to-infinitive: need to, start to, try to, hope to, decide to, plan to, seem to, learn to, manage to.
- Verb + -ing: enjoy, suggest, finish, avoid, mind, keep, practise.
- Verb + either (no change in meaning): begin, start, continue, hate, like, love, prefer.
- Expressing purpose with "to" / "in order to": "She went to the shop to buy a toothbrush."
- "He wants the teacher to tell a story." (verb + object + to-infinitive, basic).

GR-VPT-B1 | Verb Patterns — Meaning-Changing Gerund vs Infinitive Pairs | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Verbs that CHANGE MEANING depending on to-infinitive vs. -ing:
  - remember doing (past action) vs. remember to do (not forget).
  - stop doing (end an action) vs. stop to do (pause in order to do).
  - forget doing (have no memory of) vs. forget to do (fail to do).
  - regret doing (feel sorry about past) vs. regret to say (formal present).
  - try doing (experiment) vs. try to do (attempt).
- Reporting verbs with specific patterns: admit doing, refuse to do, offer to do, suggest doing, agree to do, promise to do, deny doing, warn (someone) not to do.

GR-VPT-B2 | Verb Patterns — Complex Object Patterns and Perception Verbs | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Verb + object + infinitive: "She made him cry." "I let her go." "He helped me carry the bags."
- Verb + object + -ing (perception verbs): "I saw him leaving." "She heard them shouting."
- Complex gerund and infinitive forms: perfect gerunds ("He admitted having stolen it."), passive gerunds ("She hates being ignored.").

SECTION 19: PREPOSITIONS (PRE)
================================================================================

GR-PRE-A1 | Prepositions — Place and Time | A1
Introduced at: Pre-A1
What is tested:
- Prepositions of place: in, on, at, next to, behind, in front of, under, between, opposite.
- Prepositions of time: in (months, years, seasons), on (days, dates), at (clock times, fixed points: at night, at noon).


GR-PRE-A2 | Prepositions — Movement and Prepositional Phrases | A2
Introduced at: A2
What is tested:
- Prepositions of movement: to, from, into, out of, through, past, along, across, up, down, around.
- Common prepositional phrases: by bus, on foot, in a hurry, by mistake, at the end, in the middle of.
- Preposition + noun as complement: "He's good at maths." "She's afraid of dogs." "I'm interested in art."
- Stranding (preposition at end): "What are you looking at?" "Who did you speak to?"

SECTION 20: CONNECTORS AND DISCOURSE MARKERS (CON)
================================================================================

GR-CON-A1 | Connectors — Basic Clause Joining (And, But, Because, So) | A1
Introduced at: A1
What is tested:
- And, but, or: "I've got a pen and a pencil." "She likes cats but not dogs."
- Because (basic reason): "I'm happy because it's my birthday."
- So (basic result): "It was cold so I wore a coat."
- Then (sequencing): "We ate lunch and then we played."
Note: "Conjunctions" listed explicitly.

GR-CON-B1 | Connectors — Cause, Contrast, Result and Purpose | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Contrast: although, even though, however, despite, in spite of, while, whereas.
  "Although it was raining, we went for a walk."
- Result/reason: therefore, as a result, because of, due to, since, as.
- Purpose: in order to, so that, so as to.
- Addition: furthermore, moreover, in addition, as well as.
- Condition: unless, as long as, provided that.

GR-CON-B2 | Connectors — Nuanced Logical Relations and Hedging Discourse | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Concession: nevertheless, nonetheless, even so, having said that.
- Contrast (formal): on the other hand, by contrast, conversely.
- Emphasising: above all, in particular, especially.
- Summarising/concluding: on balance, all things considered, to sum up.
- Hedging discourse: it could be argued that, to some extent, broadly speaking.

================================================================================
SECTION 21: NARRATIVE TENSES (NAR)
================================================================================

GR-NAR-B1 | Narrative Tenses — Combining Past Simple, Continuous and Perfect | B1
Introduced at: B1
What is tested:
- Using past simple, past continuous, and past perfect together to tell a story.
- Past simple: completed actions in sequence.
- Past continuous: background/ongoing actions.
- Past perfect: action that happened before the main past narrative.
- Example: "When I arrived (past simple), my friends were waiting (past continuous) because they had already ordered (past perfect)."

GR-NAR-B2 | Narrative Tenses — Extended Control with Would and Used To | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Full control of all narrative tenses in extended writing.
- "Would" for past habitual actions: "Every morning, he would wake up early and make coffee."
- "Used to" vs. "would": used to for states and habits; would only for habits (not states).
- Past perfect continuous for ongoing past action before another past event: "She had been working on the project for months before she finally finished."

================================================================================
SECTION 22: INVERSION AND ADVANCED STRUCTURES (INV)
================================================================================

GR-INV-B2 | Inversion — Negative Adverbials | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Inverted word order after negative or restrictive adverbials at the front of a sentence:
  "Never have I seen such a crowd."
  "Not only did he arrive late, but he also forgot his homework."
  "Hardly had she sat down when the phone rang."
  "Seldom do we get the chance to meet."
  "No sooner had we left than it started to rain."
- Used for emphasis and formal/literary register.

GR-CLF-B2 | Cleft Sentences | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- It-cleft: "It was John who called." "It was on Tuesday that the accident happened."
- Wh-cleft: "What I need is a holiday." "What surprised me was his reaction."
- Used to emphasise a specific element of a sentence.

GR-PTC-B2 | Participle Clauses | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- Present participle clause (simultaneous or immediately preceding action): "Walking down the street, she heard a noise." "Hearing a noise, she turned around."
- Past participle clause (passive meaning): "Written in 1900, the letter was found in the attic."
- Perfect participle clause (completed action before main verb): "Having finished her work, she went home."

GR-ELP-B2 | Ellipsis and Substitution | B2
Introduced at: B2
What is tested:
- "So do I." / "Neither do I." / "Nor have we."
- "Do so" as verb substitute: "She asked me to call and I did so."
- "One / ones": "The red one is nicer than the blue ones."
- Ellipsis of repeated verb phrases: "She can play guitar and so can her brother."
